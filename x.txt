Post 2: Why Recall.ai

The key problem:

how can I allow my meeting bot to join all major meeting platforms like Zoom, Google Meet, Microsoft Teams, and Webex?

Building each integration independently would mean dealing with:

Different APIs
Authentication
Platform-specific edge cases
Ongoing maintenance

That could essentially become an entire product by itself.

You chose @recallai because it provides a unified API and functionality such as:

Meeting bots
Audio/video recordings
Transcripts
Calendar integrations
Webhooks
Meeting metadata

You also included pricing:

5 free hours, then $0.50 per recording hour, billed by meeting duration rather than participants.

The main takeaway:

Using Recall lets me focus on what actually makes Rika unique, instead of rebuilding infrastructure that already exists.

Post 3: Why RAG instead of just summaries

The core argument:

Meeting summaries are useful until you need a specific answer.

Example questions:

"what did Rahul say about the deployment timeline?"
"why did we decide to postpone the release?"

A summary only preserves highlights.

Rika instead uses RAG so that:

Meeting transcripts are processed.
Transcripts are chunked.
Chunks are embedded.
Embeddings are stored.
Relevant pieces are retrieved when a question is asked.
The answer is generated from those retrieved transcript sections.

Core takeaway:

Summaries tell you what happened.
RAG lets you have a conversation with your meeting history.

We originally planned to follow this with a RAG architecture post, but you decided not to publish that yet because the current RAG architecture still needs improvements.

Post 5: Chunking meeting transcripts

This is the latest post we worked on.

The problem:

How do you split a meeting transcript before sending it for embeddings?

A whole meeting transcript is too large and isn't useful as one embedding.

You also don't want to blindly split every N characters/tokens because that could break a speaker's thought in the middle.

Current Rika approach:

Chunk based on speaker turns.
Cap each chunk at around 150 words.
Preserve speaker information.
Preserve timestamps.

The resulting pipeline is:

Meeting → Transcript → Chunking → Embeddings → Qdrant → Retrieval → Answer

The next natural post is about embeddings, because after creating meaningful chunks, those chunks need to be converted into vectors and stored for semantic retrieval.
